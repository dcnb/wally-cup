## Design Context

### Users
A family (the Beckers + Ro, per the README) playing together, sometimes gathered around one big screen with a single person clicking, sometimes each on a phone or tablet. Mixed ages. Sessions are casual and social: pick racers, watch the race, cheer. Tournament mode extends that into a bracket that gets raced at leisure over days, so the bracket has to be readable at a glance on both a TV and a phone.

### Brand Personality
Playful and silly. Big-event hype. Cozy and handmade.
The announcer is a goofy stadium voice: dramatic stakes, made-up car facts, and a little absurdity. The look is a printed retro Grand Prix poster: paper, hard ink shadows, stamped buttons and slightly tilted cards.

### Aesthetic Direction
- **Theme:** light "paper" UI (`--paper`, `--paper-deep`) with ink outlines and hard offset shadows. Dark ink-navy (`#1d2145`) is reserved for stages: podium, champion, Rainbow Road.
- **Palette:** tomato (`--red`), mustard, teal, ink navy, paper cream. Rainbow Road stripes are the poster palette: tomato, orange, mustard, leaf green, teal, ink blue. No violet or magenta.
- **Type:** Alfa Slab One for display, Barlow Semi Condensed for body (already in use).
- **Shapes and effects:** flat fills with ink outlines, hard offset shadows, rotated stamp-like elements.
- **Anti-references:** neon or glowing sci-fi, purple or blue gradients, dark backgrounds with glow, generic rounded-card app UI with soft shadows, cutesy or baby-ish pastel looks.
- **Bans that apply here:** no gradient text, no colored side-stripe borders, no glow or glassmorphism.

### Design Principles
1. **Poster, not app.** Hard shadows, ink outlines and paper textures beat soft shadows and generic cards.
2. **Big moments get big treatment.** Finals, champions and fireworks can be loud, but they stay inside the same palette and flat-ink language as everything else.
3. **Silly first, never childish.** Humor lives in the copy and the cars. The visuals stay confident and designed, with no babyish pastels.
4. **Works on the couch and in the hand.** Everything must read across a room on a TV and be tappable on a phone: big targets, high contrast, a bracket that scrolls sideways instead of shrinking.
5. **Accessibility defaults.** Keep contrast readable, respect `prefers-reduced-motion`, and don't rely on color alone to show who won. No specific additional needs were requested.
